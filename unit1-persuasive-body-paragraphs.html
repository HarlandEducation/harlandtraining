Persuasive Body Paragraphs

A persuasive body paragraph is a group of sentences about one topic. It can stand on its own, or appear inside an essay between the introduction and the conclusion.

When written with the correct structure, a body paragraph can be used to strongly argue almost any point, which is what makes it so powerful. For this reason, the body paragraph is one of the most influential forms of writing in the English language.

Each of these three parts is dealt with in turn in the sections ahead. To begin, here is how they look put together.

Example Paragraph Should Phones Be Allowed in the Classroom?
Students should be allowed to use phones in the classroom for three key reasons. Firstly, phones can be used to look up information. For example, a student can use a phone to research their topic when writing a research assignment without requiring the use of a personal computer. Secondly, phones can be used to record key information during class time. In this way, when the teacher is explaining a key piece of information, students can record it and listen to the recording at a later date. Finally, phones can be used to call during an emergency. A recent study showed that 80% of all emergency service calls were made from cell phones. For all these reasons, phones are a necessary tool that should be allowed in classrooms.

Video games are good for kids in three key ways. Firstly, video games help kids to improve their social connections. For example, many kids play games with other people which helps them to make new friends and improve old friendships. Secondly, video games help kids learn new skills. For instance, hand-eye coordination is improved when children play games that need fast reflexes. Finally, video games help kids by giving them an opportunity to destress. A recent study suggests that students need time to relax after a long day at school, and video games are just one way to do that. For all these reasons, video games play an important part in kids’ lives.

1.1

The Topic Sentence

The purpose of the topic sentence is to inform the reader of the one thing this paragraph is about, while expressing the writer’s belief about the topic. It must include a main topic and a topic statement, and may include a number expression. This is the most important sentence in any body paragraph — get it right, and the rest of the paragraph will practically write itself.

Main Topic
The main subject of the paragraph. Usually one or two words, and usually a noun.
Topic Statement
The one thing the writer wishes to state about the main topic — their “belief” about it.
Number Expression (optional)
The number of points the paragraph will make. Usually three works best.
Do & Don’t
DoDon’t
✓Students should be allowed to use smartphones in the classroom for three key reasons. ✕Smartphones are good for class. — too general
✓Computers are a useful tool for people’s lives in many ways. ✕Computers can be used to send emails, surf the internet, and play video games. — too specific
✓There are three must‑see places to visit when traveling in Japan. ✕Japan is nice to visit. — too general, obvious fact

Summary — The Topic Sentence

  1. Must have a main topic and a topic statement, and can include a number expression. Dolphins are amazing animals for three reasons.
  2. Can’t be too general, or the reader won’t know what the paragraph is about. Computers are great… vs. Computers are useful at school in three key ways.
  3. Can’t be too specific, or there will be nothing left to say. Japan is a great country because you can eat sushi in Tokyo… vs. Japan is a great place to go on vacation for three important reasons.
Teacher Tips
  • The main topic is usually a noun, and only one or two words.
  • A topic statement is the author’s belief about a subject.
  • A number expression says how many points will be made — e.g. “for three reasons,” “in many ways.” It isn’t required, but it can effectively guide the writing.
  • Never start a topic sentence with “I think” — it sounds weak. “I think” or “I believe” is implied but never stated.
